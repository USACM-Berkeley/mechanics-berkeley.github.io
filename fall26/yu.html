Phase Transition Meets Large Deformation: Multiphysics and Multiscale Modeling of Frozen Soils and Hydrate-Bearing Sediments

Jidu Yu, UC Berkeley Postdoctoral Researcher (advised by Prof. Kenichi Soga)
11/09, 2026 at 4:00PM-6:00PM in 3110 Etcheverry (for in-person talks) and https://berkeley.zoom.us/j/6568186472

Abstract: Frozen soils and methane hydrate-bearing sediments are granular media that undergo phase transitions, and they underlie emerging geohazards ranging from thaw-induced landslides in warming permafrost to submarine slope failures triggered by hydrate dissociation. Predicting these events is challenging because heat transfer, pore-fluid flow, phase change, and deformation are tightly coupled, material properties evolve as ice or hydrate forms or dissociates, and failure involves large deformation that is difficult to capture with mesh-based methods. In this talk, I will present a computational framework built on the material point method (MPM). First, I will introduce fully coupled thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) and thermo-hydro-mechanical-chemical (THMC) MPM formulations that account for ice-water and hydrate-gas-water phase transitions. These are solved with hybrid explicit-implicit schemes that remain stable and efficient for nearly incompressible pore fluids and low-permeability media. Second, I will present a hierarchical multiscale MPM-DEM approach, in which DEM simulations embedded at material points capture grain-scale ice bonding and hydrate cementation, removing the need for phenomenological constitutive laws. Applications include footings on thawing ground, freeze-thaw cycles, and hydrate-induced slope failure. I will close with extensions to other problems, such as 3D concrete printing.

About the speaker: Jidu Yu is a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of California, Berkeley, working with Prof. Kenichi Soga. He received his Ph.D. in Civil Engineering from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) in 2025 under the supervision of Prof. Jidong Zhao, and has been a visiting researcher at UC Berkeley and University College London. His research develops particle-based and multiscale computational methods, including MPM, DEM, and their coupling, for multiphysics problems in geomaterials under extreme environments and in fresh concrete, such as 3D concrete printing. His work has appeared in JMPS, CMAME, IJNME, and other leading journals.
